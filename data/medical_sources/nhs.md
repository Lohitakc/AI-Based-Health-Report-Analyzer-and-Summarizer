# NHS Guidance Notes

source: NHS
url: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/blood-tests/

NHS explains blood test interpretation should use the laboratory reference interval and patient context. Out-of-range values do not always mean disease but need clinical interpretation.

source: NHS
url: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/underactive-thyroid-hypothyroidism/diagnosis/

NHS thyroid guidance uses TSH with thyroid hormones (T3/T4) for thyroid evaluation. Persistently abnormal results usually require repeat testing and clinician follow-up.

source: NHS
url: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/urine-test/

NHS urinalysis material describes protein, glucose, and white cell findings as useful screening signals for kidney disease, diabetes, or urinary infection when correlated clinically.

source: NHS
url: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/urinary-tract-infections-utis/

NHS notes that urine inflammatory cells and symptoms together can indicate UTI risk and may need medical evaluation.
